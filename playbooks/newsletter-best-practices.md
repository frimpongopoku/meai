# Newsletter Best Practices

MassEnergize community newsletters are sent by volunteer organizers to their neighbors. They should feel like a friendly note from a knowledgeable neighbor, not corporate communications.

## Voice and tone

- Warm, neighborly, never preachy
- Specific, never vague — name the action, name the next step
- Acknowledge effort. Climate action is hard; people are doing their best
- Avoid jargon. "Heat pump" is fine; "thermal energy transfer device" is not

## Structure

A typical monthly or biweekly newsletter has:

1. **A short intro** — 2-3 sentences, conversational. Set context for the season or recent community win
2. **Two or three featured actions** — each with a clear "what to do next" call to action and a link
3. **A testimonial** — let a neighbor's experience do the persuading
4. **Upcoming events** — keep it brief; bullet list with date and link
5. **A short closing** — gratitude, next month preview, or a thought-provoking question

## Calls to action

Every featured action needs a specific next step. Bad: "Learn about heat pumps." Good: "Get a free home energy assessment at masssave.com."

## Subject lines

Subject lines should:
- Be 6-9 words
- Include a specific verb or hook ("Save $200 this winter," "What's new in October")
- Avoid all-caps, exclamation points, and "newsletter" in the subject
